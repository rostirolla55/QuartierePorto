Artistic and Architectural Details

  • The Artwork: A shrine dedicated to the cult of the Virgin Mary. While many Bolognese shrines feature the iconography of the Madonna di San Luca, this piece stands out for the remarkable craft of its architectural frame.

  • Architecture and Historical Revival: The facade holding the shrine was designed by architect Edoardo Collamarini and built by Alfonso Rubbiani, key figures of historical restoration and Neo-Medieval revival in Bologna in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

  • Analysis of the Image and Visible Features:

    • The Subject: Inside the niche is a refined ceramic roundel (in the Della Robbia style or a faithful reproduction) depicting the Madonna and Child, framed by a colorful garland of flowers and fruit.

    • The Inscription: Above the arch of the niche, a partially legible ancient inscription reads: "COMPAGNIA DELL'AVESELLA". This confirms the historical link to the confraternity of the same name, which managed the nearby Hospital and Oratory of Santa Maria della Pioggia.

    • The Empty Niche on the Corner: On the left, directly on the corner of the wall, is a smaller, rustic stone niche, currently empty. In Bologna, building corners frequently featured multiple devotional spots, which often doubled as holding places for oil lamps prior to public street lighting.

    • Current Condition: The image highlights a striking contrast between historical devotion (red flowers placed by local residents) and the modern urban environment, marked by graffiti on the lower section of the wall.
