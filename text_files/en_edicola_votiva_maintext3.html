Historical Insights, Anecdotes, and Curiosities

  1. The Compagnia dell'Avesella: This lay brotherhood was one of the most active in the area. The inscription above the shrine served as a formal ownership marker, reminding passersby of the spiritual protection and material aid (shelter for pilgrims and care for the poor) provided at the neighboring Hospital and Oratory of Santa Maria della Pioggia.

  2. The Hidden Canal and the Washerwomen: Today Via Avesella appears as a dry alley, but beneath the pavement flows the Avesa stream (Avesella canal). In the past, daily life here was defined by watermills, dyers, and washerwomen working along the open canal. The votive shrine offered spiritual comfort to workers in a damp area prone to frequent flooding.

  3. A Haven for Master Sculptors: Following the dissolution of religious orders by Napoleon in the late 18th century, the building's interior became prestigious sculpture studios. The area between Via Tanari and Via Avesella served for decades as the heart of 19th-century Bolognese sculpture, housing workshops of renowned masters such as Enrico Barberi and Tullo Golfarelli. Today, behind these doors lie restoration labs, exhibition spaces, and courtyards that retain the charm of old artist workshops.

  4. The "Mysterious" Niche: The smaller empty niche on the corner likely held a simpler terracotta figurine, known locally as a "Earthquake Madonna" or protector of travelers, which was lost or removed over time.

  5. Another Votive Shrine Nearby: Demonstrating the deep popular devotion of this street, another votive shrine depicting St. Anthony with Child is located nearby at Via Avesella 28.
