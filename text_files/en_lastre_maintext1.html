Walking through the historical center of Bologna, one often notices peculiar sandstone and terracotta plaques embedded in the walls beside doorways. These are not mere decorations, but tangible remnants of an urban management revolution.

History and Origin of House Numbering

  • The Pre-Napoleonic Era: Before the late 18th century, houses in Bologna had no street numbers. Orientation relied on informal landmarks: family names, shop signs, wall frescoes, or distinctive facade features.

  • Late 18th-Century Reform: Under Napoleonic rule (1795–1796), a systematic house-numbering system was introduced to improve postal delivery, tax administration, and civic organization.

  • Comparing Materials (Sandstone vs. Terracotta): The earliest plaques were hand-carved in sandstone (arenaria), the traditional stone from the local hills. Hand-engraving stone was a skilled craft aimed at durability. Over time, terracotta plaques became widely used as a cheaper, mass-produced alternative to replace worn stones or fit new buildings.
