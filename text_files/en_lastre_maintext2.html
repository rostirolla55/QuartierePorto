The Case of Via Tanari Vecchia 11 (former No. 1202)

A clear example of this historical shift is found in Via Tanari Vecchia:

  • Next to current house number 11 (an odd number), an original sandstone plaque reading number 1202 (an even number) is still clearly preserved.

  • Why the Discrepancy Between Even and Odd? In the original Napoleonic system, numbers were not separated into even (right) and odd (left) sides per street. Instead, numbers followed a single continuous sequence through an entire urban sector. Only modern street-naming rules introduced the current layout: increasing numbers with odd on the left and even on the right along the direction of travel.

Anecdotes, Trivia, and Insights

  1. Sequential Numbers into the Thousands: Under the earliest system, houses were numbered sequentially up to several thousands per city sector (Sestiere), rather than starting from 1 on each street. This explains why a small alley like Via Tanari Vecchia displays a number as high as 1202.

  2. Citizen Suspicion: Local residents initially distrusted the new house numbers, viewing them as a tool for military conscription and higher taxation. Some homeowners even attempted to plaster over the stone plaques.

  3. Endurance Under the Porticoes: The sandstone was sourced from the nearby Apennine quarries. Protected beneath Bologna’s sheltered porticoes, these hand-carved stone plaques have survived intact for more than two centuries.
